one-time SSP churns ~25–40%/yr without it). Present Managed as the default "and then we keep it current," and
Guided as the premium done-with-you flagship.

## Closing flexibility — value, not an advertised discount (founder discretion)
- The list price is the list price. For the first few founding-cohort deals you may flex **setup** (a closing
  lever, 1:1 only — never published, never "10% off" language). Keep **recurring at standard** so you're
  validating real willingness to pay, not buying logos.
- Cap concurrent onboarding at review capacity (~3–5/wk). Beyond that, waitlist at standard pricing — scarcity
  ("limited CMMC-RP slots") is the sell, not a discount.

## "How we quote" rubric (the published price is the start; confirm scope, then lock the fixed number)
1. Confirm **scope** from intake: number of systems/boundaries, stack (inheritance reduces work), how much
   existing documentation can be imported.
2. Confirm **timeline** vs. the deadline. Tight timelines = Complete/Managed/Guided, not Starter.
3. Confirm **who signs** and that they're bought in.
4. Map to a tier; confirm the **fixed number** (the published price, adjusted only for genuine scope) — no hourly meter.
5. **Check review capacity** before promising a turnaround — never sell a date Brian can't review by.
6. Default the proposal to **Complete/Managed/Guided**; offer Starter only as a down-sell to keep a deal alive.

## What NOT to do
- Don't advertise the 10% cut as a discount — it's the new list price. Cold copy still leads with value, not a number.
- Don't quote below the published list to win a deal (flex **setup** 1:1 if you must; protect recurring).
- Don't promise a turnaround you can't expert-review in time.
- Don't claim certification, FedRAMP, CUI hosting, **"independent measurement,"** or anything in §12's "never"
  list to justify price. An RP advises; a C3PAO assesses.
