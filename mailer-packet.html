The reality, honestly

What you're actually signing — and why the number matters.

1 · You sign it. Personally. Under penalty of law.

The CMMC affirmation isn't a form your IT person files. A senior official attests in SPRS that the company meets the requirements — it's non-delegable, and it carries False Claims Act exposure. "Knowing" under the FCA includes reckless disregard — signing a number you never actually verified.

The DOJ is collecting at record scale — $6.8B in False Claims Act recoveries in FY2025, the most in the law's history, and roughly $5.3B of it driven by whistleblowers. Recent settlements for misstated cybersecurity compliance: Georgia Tech — $875K (reported by the company's own cybersecurity team), MORSECORP — $4.6M (a false "−142" SPRS score), Raytheon — $8.4M. Penalties run to treble damages — and the whistleblower is often an insider who already knows your real number.

2 · The SPRS math is unforgiving.

You don't earn points up from zero. You start at 110 and lose 5, 3, or 1 point for every unmet control, down to a −203 floor. So a shop that feels "70% there" often has a negative posted score. This isn't hypothetical: in June 2026 a Navy contractor that had posted a perfect 110 settled with the DOJ for $507,144 after a government review put its real score at −170 — a 280-point gap, with no data breach ever alleged. The number you post is the number you're liable for.

The trap that fails assessments: certain controls are "must-meet" — they cannot go on a POA&M. Leave one open and you fail outright, regardless of score. Anything you do defer must close within 180 days. Most shops learn which controls are non-negotiable the expensive way — from a C3PAO.

3 · The clock — and the bottleneck.

CMMC Phase 2 (Level 2 certification by an independent C3PAO) phases into new DoD contracts starting November 10, 2026 — and a prime can ask for your self-assessed SPRS score today, as a condition of staying on the bid list.

~100 authorized assessors. Tens of thousands of contractors who'll need them. Readiness work takes months, and the assessor calendar fills first-come. Knowing your real number now is the difference between getting in line and getting left out.

Sources: 32 CFR Part 170 & DFARS 252.204-7021; NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 DoD Assessment Methodology; DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative settlement releases. Educational, not legal advice.
